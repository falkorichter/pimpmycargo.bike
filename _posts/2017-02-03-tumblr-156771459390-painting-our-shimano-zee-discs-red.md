<h2>Painting our Shimano Zee discs RED</h2>
<p>As mentioned before, I wanted disc brakes with red discs matching the red hydraulic wires and the red seat belts.</p>
<p>Since the original Shimano Zee discs were only available in black, I had to make them <strong>RED</strong> myself. Bought some red glossy spray paint today and went at it:</p>
<p><strong>First step:</strong> Masked the discs. Using normal masking tape</p>
<p><strong>Second step: </strong>Sand papered (is that a thing?) the disc:</p>
<p><strong>Third step:</strong> First attempt on spray painting the discs:</p>
<p><strong>Fourth step:</strong> realized this layer of paint was lousy. I accidentally watched a Youtube video which turned out to be very helpful.</p>
<p><strong>Fifth step:</strong> Sanded the first layer of paint off and followed the tips of Adam Savage from the Youtube video. The <strong>second attempt</strong> of spray painting was much better. I painted the front of the disc from the top, rotated it 180 degrees and painted the back. This way not only the facing sides of the discs were painted evenly, but also the inner sides. I was also much more conservative, only spraying super thin layers. After each layer I used my wife&#x27;s <strong>hairdryer</strong> to dry the paint. I was able to add allot of layers of paint in a very short time.</p>
<p><strong>Images of the discs after the final paint:</strong></p>
<p>The paint job on the inside was quite good. There is some traces of paint on the outside, but I´m sure they will rub off from the brakes.</p>
<p>It looked quite cool actually after I sanded off the first lousy layer of paint. <em>Shabby chic</em> <strong>used</strong> look. Unfortunately I was too enthusiastic and forgot to take a picture of the shabby chic brakes. Maybe I will be forced to go back to that look because the paint will not stay on the disc when riding the bike through the <strong>dirty streets of Berlin</strong>.</p>
